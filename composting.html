FARM & LARGE GARDEN SCALE

1. Indore Aerobic Heap / Pit Method

Developed by Sir Albert Howard and Yashwant D. Wad at the Institute of Plant Industry, Indore.

TIME TO HARVEST 90 – 120 Days

The 4 Essential Steps:

1

Site & Foundation

Mark an 8×4 ft shaded, elevated ground or excavate a 3-ft deep pit sheltered from torrential monsoon downpours.

2

Layered Filling

Layer 6 inches dry stalks/leaves (browns), 3 inches fresh green waste, and 2 inches cattle dung slurry with fine garden soil.

3

Moisture & Aeration

Keep pile damp (50–60% moisture like a wrung-out sponge). Insert vertical bamboo poles with drilled holes for core airflow.

4

Systematic Turning

Turn the pile thoroughly at Day 15, Day 30, and Day 60 to ensure uniform thermophilic breakdown. Harvest rich crumbly humus.

✅ Best Practices (Do's)

  • Chop thick crop stalks (cotton/maize) into 2-inch pieces before loading.
  • Keep the heap dome-shaped to shed excessive seasonal rain.
  • Add old ripe compost or forest soil as a microbial inoculum.

❌ Avoid (Don'ts)

  • Do not add plastic, pesticide-sprayed foliage, or sick diseased plants.
  • Never allow the heap to dry out completely or become waterlogged.
COMMUNITY & HIGH VOLUME FARM SCALE

2. NADEP Brick Tank Composting

Designed by Gandhian agriculturist Narayan Deotao Pandharipande (Kaka) in Maharashtra. Generates 3 tons of compost per cycle.

TIME TO HARVEST 110 – 120 Days

The 4 Essential Steps:

1

Honeycomb Tank

Construct a permanent brick tank (10×5×3 ft). Leave 7-inch air ventilation gaps in every third brick course for permanent passive aeration.

2

Substrate Ratio Formula

Load 1,400 kg agricultural waste + 100 kg cow dung (in 1,500L water slurry) + 1,750 kg fine sieved farm soil in alternating layers.

3

Dome & Plaster Seal

Heap the final layer 1.5 ft above the brim like a rounded cone. Seal completely with a 3-inch mud-cow dung plaster to retain internal heat.

4

Zero-Turning Maturation

Repair cracks with mud slurry. In 120 days without physical turning, excavate 2.5–3 tons of rich black organic manure ready for fields.

NUTRIENT-DENSE VERMICAST & VERMIWASH

3. Eisenia Fetida Vermicomposting

Harnessing epigeic Australian Red Earthworms to produce plant hormones, enzymes, and granular castings.

TIME TO HARVEST 40 – 45 Days

The 4 Essential Steps:

1

Bedding Preparation

Prepare a shaded concrete or HDPE bed (3 ft width, 1.5 ft height). Lay 3 inches dry coconut coir or straw for drainage.

2

Pre-Digested Substrate

Feed only 10–14 day pre-decomposed cattle dung and soft crop leaves. Raw dung produces fatal heat (thermophilic spike) that kills worms.

3

Worm Inoculation

Release 1,000–2,000 Eisenia fetida worms per 10 sq ft. Cover bed with damp gunny bags and maintain 60% moisture in cool shade.

4

Castings & Vermiwash

Collect brown liquid Vermiwash from bottom drainage tap. Stop watering 3 days before harvest so worms descend; scrape granular top castings.

✅ Earthworm Diet Favorites

  • Semi-decomposed cow/buffalo dung, tender neem leaves, spent banana skins, and tea grounds.
  • Moist gunny bags to keep bed temperature at 20°C–30°C.

❌ Strictly Prohibited

  • Citrus peels (lemon/orange), onions, garlic, green chilies, oils, vinegar, and animal meats.
  • Direct scorching sunlight or dry bedding.
APARTMENTS & URBAN GARDENS

4. Aerobic Kitchen Pot & Balcony Composting

Zero-odor indoor and terrace system tailored for apartments and urban families.

TIME TO HARVEST 45 – 60 Days
1

3-Tier Terracotta Pots

Use three stacked porous clay pots (Khamba) with drainage holes allowing air exchange and moisture balance.

2

Chop & Layer

Chop vegetable peels into 1-inch bits. For every 1 cup of wet scraps, add 2 cups of dry coco-peat or crushed leaves.

3

Microbial Kickstart

Add a spoonful of sour curd water, buttermilk, or old compost once a week to seed beneficial lactic acid bacteria.

4

Harvest for Pots

When top pot fills, rotate to bottom. After 45–60 days, dark, fragrant compost is ready for potted plants.

Modern & Biological Solutions

Bokashi, Green Manure & Biochar

Accelerated anaerobic fermentation, legume cover cropping, and permanent soil carbon anchors.

🪣

5. Bokashi Anaerobic Fermentation

Japanese anaerobic pickling method. Food waste (including cooked food) is layered with Effective Microorganisms (EM) bran in an airtight bucket. Produces potent liquid foliar tea in days and pickles waste for rapid soil burial.

Key Advantage: Can ferment all cooked foods without attracting flies.
🌱

Green Manuring (~45 Days)

Fast-growing legumes like Dhaincha (Sesbania) or Sunhemp sown at onset of monsoons and ploughed back into soil at flowering stage (40–45 days). Fixes up to 80–100 kg atmospheric nitrogen per hectare naturally.

Key Advantage: Drastically reduces reliance on purchased nitrogen inputs.
🔥

Biochar Carbon Anchor

Porous carbon created via low-oxygen pyrolysis of agricultural crop stalks. When inoculated with Jeevamrut or compost tea, biochar provides permanent microscopic microbial condominiums that stay in soil for centuries.

Key Advantage: Boosts soil water retention and prevents nutrient leaching.

Practical Diagnostics

Compost Troubleshooting: Problem ➜ Fix

Quick scientific remedies for common composting hurdles.

👃

Foul / Rotten Egg Smell

Cause: Anaerobic conditions or excess wet greens.

Fix: Immediately add dry crushed brown leaves, straw, or sawdust and thoroughly turn the pile to introduce oxygen.

🏜️

Dry & Cold Center

Cause: Inadequate moisture or insufficient nitrogen.

Fix: Sprinkle water or cow dung slurry evenly while turning so moisture reaches 50–60%. Cover pile to conserve heat.

🪰

Flies or Maggots

Cause: Exposed food scraps on top of pile.

Fix: Always bury fresh wet scraps deep inside the center and cap the top with 2–3 inches of dry soil or brown leaves.

⏳

Slow Decay / No Breakdown

Cause: Waste particles too large or lack of microbial starter.

Fix: Chop stalks into 1–2 inch pieces. Add 500ml sour buttermilk or 2 kg fresh cow dung slurry to inoculate bacteria.
