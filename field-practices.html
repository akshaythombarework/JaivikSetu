Agroecological Management

Sustainable Field Practices

Biological systems replace chemical interventions through smart crop geometry, organic soil blankets, pulse nitrogen fixation, and precision water drops.

PRACTICE 01

Legume Crop Rotation & Soil Nitrogen

Monoculture depletes specific soil nutrient horizons and creates rampant pest reservoirs. Rotating heavy feeders (cotton, maize, wheat) with symbiotic nitrogen-fixing legumes (chickpea, pigeon pea, green gram, cowpea) breaks pest lifecycles and deposits up to 50–70 kg atmospheric nitrogen per hectare into the soil without a single bag of synthetic urea.

🌿 Breaks Pest Cycles 🌱 Biological Nitrogen Fixation 💰 Eliminates Urea Purchase 📉 Improves Soil Aggregates
Recommended Rotation Sequence:
  • Kharif Season: Cotton / Soybean intercropped with Pigeon Pea (Tur).
  • Rabi Season: Chickpea (Chana) or Wheat with Mustard borders.
  • Summer Fallow: Fast 45-day Green Manure (Dhaincha / Sunhemp).
PRACTICE 02

Multispecies Intercropping & Trap Crops

Planting complementary companion species simultaneously in alternating field rows maximizes solar harvest and provides biological pest barriers. Tall crops like maize or sorghum act as windbreaks and physical barricades against flying aphids, while fragrant marigolds release root exudates that paralyze harmful parasitic nematodes.

🌻 Natural Trap Cropping ☀️ 100% Sunlight Capture 🐝 Attracts Beneficial Pollinators 🛡️ Biological Insurance Against Crop Failure
Proven Companion Combinations:
  • Cotton + Cowpea (4:1): Cowpea attracts ladybird beetles that eat cotton bollworms.
  • Tomato + African Marigold (8:1): Marigold acts as decoy for fruit borers.
  • Maize + Cowpea + Pumpkin: The ancient "Three Sisters" synergy.
PRACTICE 03

Organic Mulching (Soil Living Blanket)

Bare soil is an open wound. Covering farm beds with a 3–4 inch protective blanket of dried crop straw, sugarcane trash, or dry tree leaves keeps soil temperature 5°C cooler in summer heat, reduces soil water evaporation by over 60%, and completely smothers weed seeds by blocking sunlight.

💧 60% Water Conservation 🚫 Complete Weed Suppression 🌡️ Prevents Soil Heat Stress 🪱 Earthworm Highway Habitat
Mulching Golden Rules:
  • Keep mulch 2 inches away from plant stems to avoid stem rot.
  • Spray Jeevamrut over dry mulch to speed up decomposition into humus.
  • Use on-farm crop stalks rather than burning fields (anti-stubble burning).
PRACTICE 04

Precision Drip Irrigation & Water Care

Flood irrigation causes topsoil erosion, anaerobic suffocating waterlogging, and excessive weed outbreaks in field furrows. Precision drip delivery applies micro-metered droplets directly to the active plant rhizosphere, cutting water consumption by 50% while allowing direct fertigation of filtered Vermiwash and liquid bio-fertilizers.

💦 50% Water Savings 🌾 Fertigation of Organic Teas 🚫 Zero Furrow Weed Surge ⚡ Lower Pumping Electricity
Organic Fertigation Protocol:
  • Filter Jeevamrut or Vermiwash through 3 layers of fine muslin cloth.
  • Flush drip lateral lines with clean water for 15 minutes after foliar dosing.
  • Avail 55–80% government subsidy under PMKSY (Per Drop More Crop).
PRACTICE 05

Heirloom Desi Seeds & Beejamrut Inoculation

Hybrid seeds are bred for synthetic chemical responsiveness and cannot be safely re-sown. Traditional heirloom open-pollinated desi seeds possess centuries of acclimatization against local droughts, pest outbreaks, and heat spells. Coating seeds with cow-dung Beejamrut before sowing inoculates the seedling with protective beneficial endophytes.

🌰 100% Seed Sovereignty 🛡️ Natural Climate Hardiness 🦠 Beejamrut Biological Shield 🌾 Rich Indigenous Nutrition & Taste
Beejamrut Seed Treatment:

Mix 5 kg cow dung, 5L cow urine, 50g virgin forest lime, and handful of virgin soil in 20L water. Smear gently onto seeds with bare hands. Dry in shade for 2 hours before sowing.
