Fundamental Knowledge

What is Organic Farming?

A production system that sustains the health of soils, ecosystems, and people. It relies on ecological processes, biodiversity, and cycles adapted to local conditions, rather than synthetic inputs with adverse effects.

National Context

6 Verified Facts on Organic Farming in India

Key data verified through APEDA, National Programme for Organic Production (NPOP), and Ministry of Agriculture.

🥇

1. World's #1 in Organic Farmers

India is home to the largest number of certified organic producers globally, with over 4.4 million farmers registered under PGS-India and NPOP certification systems.

🏔️

2. Sikkim: World's 1st 100% Organic State

In 2016, Sikkim became the first state in the world to successfully convert 100% of its farmland (over 76,000 hectares) to certified chemical-free organic farming, winning the UN Future Policy Gold Award.

📜

3. Dual Certification Architecture

India established NPOP (2001) under the Ministry of Commerce for international export equivalence, and PGS-India (2011) under the Ministry of Agriculture for affordable peer certification for local farmers.

🗺️

4. Leading Organic States

Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, and Gujarat represent over 60% of India's total certified organic cultivable area, led by soybean, cotton, pulses, and wheat production.

🚢

5. Major Global Export Commodities

India exports organic certified products to the USA, EU, Canada, and Great Britain. Leading exports include organic oilseeds (sesame and soybean meal), tea, medicinal herbs, spices, and basmati rice.

📈

6. Immense Expansion Frontier

While India leads in farmer count, organic farming covers approximately 3–4% of India's net sown area, presenting a massive growth horizon under the National Mission on Natural Farming.

Humus & Decomposed Biomass (0 – 2 inches)

The topmost organic cushion created by decayed leaves, compost, and earthworm castings. Contains high biological moisture, stores active carbon, and acts as a climate sponge against rainfall erosion.

Microbial Life & Inhabitants: Fungi mycelium, aerobic microbes, protozoa, and micro-arthropods.
Ecological Role: Absorbs 90% of rainwater impact and prevents hardpan soil compaction.

Warning Signs

Chemical Degradation: Acidity, Salinity & Hardpan

Decades of continuous urea, DAP, and chemical pesticide applications trigger catastrophic physiological changes in farm soils:

  • ✖ Soil Acidification & Burn: Excess synthetic ammonium fertilizers release hydrogen ions, dropping soil pH below 5.5 and locking vital phosphorus away from crops.
  • ✖ Salinity Crusts: Unabsorbed chemical salts crystallize on the surface, drawing moisture out of plant roots via reverse osmosis.
  • ✖ Impermeable Hardpan: Without organic matter and earthworms, subsoil aggregates fuse into a dense concrete-like layer that blocks deep root penetration.

Simple Farm Soil Testing Tips

Before starting organic transition, evaluate your field's baseline biological indicators:

1. pH Indicator Test: Ideal organic soil ranges from 6.5 to 7.5. Acidic soils benefit from wood ash and lime; saline soils require green manuring with Dhaincha.
2. Soil Organic Carbon (SOC): Healthy organic soil should have > 0.8% organic carbon. Most chemically exhausted Indian soils test at only 0.2–0.4%.
3. Earthworm Census: Dig a 1 cubic foot hole. Counting 5–10 active earthworms confirms a vibrant, recovering living soil.
